# TMM research and design result

## Problem interpretation

Design a shared front-end infrared filter coating for gas turbine and industrial flue gas online monitoring systems. The detector needs to transmit radiation in two narrow bands: CO at 4.55–4.75 μm and unspecified material at 4.15–4.35 μm, while suppressing background and other thermal radiation in the ranges 3.60–4.00 μm and 4.85–5.20 μm. Design a planar multilayer film under unspecified material on a CaF2 substrate, using no metal layers, only conventional depositable infrared inorganic dielectric materials, with a total layer count not exceeding 24 layers.

## Method research

- **Quarter-wave stack with computational optimization**: Start with alternating quarter-wave layers of high and low index transparent materials to form Fabry-Perot cavities, then use computational optimization to adjust layer thicknesses for target bandwidth and out-of-band blocking. Multiple cavities can be coupled in series to shape the passband profile from triangular to square.
- **Spectral matching optimization for gas absorption bands**: Filter transmittance should maximize over the target gas-absorbing spectral region while minimizing transmittance over absorbing regions of interfering gases and water vapor. Bandwidth selection trades off between suppressing spectral cross-interference (narrower) and maintaining signal intensity (wider), and should match the characteristic absorption peak width of the target gas.
- **High-low index material pair selection**: Select high-index and low-index dielectric materials with significant refractive index difference, low absorption coefficient in the target wavelength range, and matching stress properties. This combination decreases required film thickness, increases maximum transmittance, and improves filter stability.
- **Reflector type selection for transmission optimization**: Dielectric Bragg reflectors achieve higher peak transmissivity than metal-based reflectors in the infrared waveband. Metal layers can reduce unwanted reflections from rejected bands but introduce 15-30 percent absorption loss. Design choice depends on whether maximum transmission or simplified stack thickness is prioritized.
