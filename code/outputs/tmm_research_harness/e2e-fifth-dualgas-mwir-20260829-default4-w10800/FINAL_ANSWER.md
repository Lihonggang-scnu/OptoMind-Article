# TMM research and design result

## Problem interpretation

Design a shared front-end filter coating for a dual-gas remote sensing payload on a small satellite. The sensor requires transmission of radiation in two narrow bands: Methane 3.25-3.35 um and Carbon Dioxide 4.20-4.30 um, while suppressing solar and Earth background in 2.50-3.10 um and 4.45-5.00 um. Design a planar multilayer film under unspecified material incidence and CaF2 substrate conditions, containing no metal layers, using only conventional depositable infrared inorganic dielectric materials, with a total layer count not exceeding 24 layers.

## Method research

- **Alternating Index Multilayer**: Construct filters from alternating high and low index dielectric materials, each layer at quarter wavelength optical thickness. More layers produce steeper spectral slopes and higher out-of-band blocking, though transmission may drop and passbands acquire spectral ripple as thickness increases.
- **Long-Pass Short-Pass Combination**: Construct bandpass filters by combining long wave-pass and short wave-pass filter sections. This approach is suitable for broadest bandpass filters where transmission is limited on either side by rejection regions.
- **Geometric Mean Index Quarter-Wave**: For single-frequency or narrow-range AR coating, use material with refractive index equal to geometric mean of incident and substrate refractive indices, with thickness equal to one-quarter the in-material wavelength at target frequency. Can be extended to multiple dielectric layers for broader bandwidth.
- **TMM-Based Dual-Objective Optimization**: Use transfer matrix method in optimization code to balance competing objectives such as maximum transmission versus minimum FWHM. Layer thicknesses are optimization variables verified by electromagnetic simulations. Consider thermal stress and deposition-induced effects in design process.
- **Manufacturability-Limited Layer Count**: Impose total coating thickness constraints during optimization to ensure manufacturability. Infrared coatings limited to approximately 40 micrometers, visible to 20 micrometers. Thicker coatings increase vacuum chamber residence time and make transmissive behavior harder to control with real-time quality control.
- **Center Wavelength Bandwidth Optimization**: For gas sensing applications, optimize bandpass filter for center wavelength and bandwidth as critical parameters while allowing minor transmittance deviation. Filter transmittance must maximize over gas-absorbing spectral region and approach zero over absorbing regions of interfering gases.
