# TMM research and design result

## Problem interpretation

Design a planar multilayer dielectric film for a spaceborne quantum key distribution receiver protective window. Incident medium: unspecified material. Substrate: Fused Silica. Materials: HfO2 and SiO2 only. Total layers <= 30. Objectives: Maximize transmittance in 1530-1565 nm band for signal reception. Maximize reflectance in 400-700 nm band to suppress solar background.

## Method research

- **Modified quarter-wave optical thickness design**: Use quarter-wave optical thickness (lambda0/(4*n)) layers as building blocks for high-reflection mirrors, with modified outer layer thickness (such as double quarter-wave of low-index material or lambda0/8) to improve environmental durability and spectral performance. Layer sequence starts with high-index material on substrate and ends with low-index material.
- **Genetic algorithm for multilayer parameter optimization**: Apply evolutionary optimization with selection, mutation, and crossover operations to optimize both layer thicknesses and material types simultaneously. Index material candidates for optimization and expand design space beyond fixed material assignments. Iteratively improve solutions through generations while respecting layer count constraints.
- **All-dielectric Fabry-Perot bandpass structure**: Replace metallic reflecting layers with all-dielectric multilayer mirrors forming a Fabry-Perot interferometer structure. Use dielectric mirrors (9 or more layers each) separated by a resonance cavity layer with optical thickness of half-wavelength. Multiple cavities can be coupled in series to modify pass band shape from triangular to smoother profiles.
- **Interlayer graded index structure**: Incorporate interlayers between discontinuous high-low index interfaces to create graded refractive index profiles. Vary intermediate layer composition through distinct cycle ratios of high and low index materials (such as HfO2 and SiO2). This reduces interface discontinuities compared to conventional multilayer stacks.
- **Needle optimization technique for non-quarter-wave designs**: Use needle optimization technique incorporated into thin-film design software to optimize non-quarter-wave optical thickness designs for multiple wavelength targets simultaneously. Define performance targets for each working wavelength with minimum band width specifications. Include environmental durability constraints such as outermost layer material and minimum thickness.
- **Antireflection coating for coupled cavity structures**: Add antireflection coatings to reduce reflectance oscillations in pass bands of coupled cavity structures. AR coating thickness equals half the dielectric spacer thickness and uses the same material. This doubles overall transmittance and smooths oscillations associated with coupled cavity modes, reducing reflectance variation in pass band.
