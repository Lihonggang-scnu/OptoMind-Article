# TMM research and design result

## Problem interpretation

Design an absorption film for a spectrally selective surface for visible light heat-absorbing coating: maximize absorptance (minimize reflectance) in the 400-700 nm visible band, and maximize reflectance in the 700-2000 nm near-infrared band to suppress heat input. Conditions: unspecified material incidence, unspecified material substrate. Use only HfO2, SiO2, and metal Cr to design a planar multilayer film. Total layer count must not exceed 20 layers.

## Method research

- **Admittance Locus Method**: Track optical admittance during layer-by-layer deposition by calculating equivalent admittance as each layer thickness increases from 0 to final value. The admittance locus includes phase thickness and optical constant of deposited film, enabling prediction of reflectance spectrum when refractive index and physical thickness are known. For opaque coatings on metallic substrates, transmittance is zero and absorptivity is deduced from reflectance calculation.
- **Multi-Island Genetic Algorithm**: Use multi-island genetic algorithm to optimize configuration of selective solar absorbers. Define spectrally averaged reflectance and absorptance through spectrum-weighted integration. The optimization treats layer configuration as variables to maximize solar absorptance while minimizing thermal emittance.
- **DMD Interference Design**: Implement dielectric/metal/dielectric (D/M/D) multilayer coating structure for spectral selectivity. The metal layer provides infrared reflectance while dielectric layers control visible-range interference. This structure achieves high absorptance in solar range and low emittance in infrared range through interference effects.
- **Protected Interference Structure**: Bury wavelength-selective interference layers below a thick hard coating layer (~2 micron) with single air-side SiO2 layer for antireflection. This approach controls oscillations in reflectance and transmittance while maximizing mechanical hardness. The buried active layers maintain optical function while being protected from surface damage.
- **Optical Constant Gradation**: Achieve enhanced absorption through gradation of optical constants across multilayer stack. Vary reactive gas flow rates, sputtering power, deposition time and thicknesses of individual layers to tune optical properties. This creates smooth transition in refractive index and absorption coefficient through the stack depth.
- **Structured Design Procedure**: Follow five-step optimization procedure: (1) setting design parameters, (2) constructing basic structures, (3) selecting thin film materials, (4) design optimization, (5) simulation and analysis. Proper difference in refractive index between materials can reduce layer count and improve durability. Design should meet spectral characteristic requirements for target wavelength bands.
- **Weighted Spectrum Integration**: Calculate solar absorptance by weighting reflection spectrum against solar radiation spectrum (ASTM G173-03). Thermal emittance calculated using black body radiation function at specified temperature (e.g., 100 C for low-temperature systems). For opaque coatings on metallic substrates, transmittance assumed zero. Ideal selective coating reflects least in 300-2500 nm range and reflects highly in 2500-15000 nm range.
- **Absorber-Reflector Tandem**: Realize spectral selectivity with two-layer structure where each layer has unique optical properties. Top layer is absorbing coating transparent to infrared irradiation. Substrate provides high infrared reflectance. Transition from low reflectance to high reflectance occurs at cutoff wavelength around 2.5 micrometer for solar thermal applications.
