# TMM research and design result

## Problem interpretation

Design a front-end filter coating for a solar-blind UV ozone and high-altitude combustion plume detector on a low-orbit space platform. The detector needs to maximize transmission of the 255–280 nm solar-blind UV signal while suppressing the 300–700 nm visible background and 700–1100 nm near-infrared stray light. Design a planar multilayer film under unspecified material incidence on a fused silica substrate, containing no metal layers, using only conventional depositable inorganic dielectric materials, with a total layer count not exceeding 30 layers.

## Method research

- **Periodic unit cell with AR coatings**: Structure filter as periodic (L/2 H L/2) m units sandwiched between two anti-reflection coatings. This symmetric unit cell approach creates a stopband with high reflectivity while maintaining transmissivity in the target passband. The AR coatings reduce interface reflections at the passband wavelengths.
- **Transparent metal with dielectric spacers**: Use metal layers separated by transparent dielectric spacers to achieve out-of-band suppression while preserving in-band UV transmission. Transparent metals can ensure transmission decrease of several orders of magnitude in visible and infrared while preserving ultraviolet transparency. Can be combined with dielectric AR coating layers.
- **Alternating index optimization with software**: Design filters using alternate stacks of thin films with alternately low and high refractive indices. Use specialized filter design software to address complexity of optimization. Software-based approach enables re-optimization for different coating or manufacturing technologies and facilitates collaboration between design groups.
- **Theoretical tolerance band incorporation**: Include theoretical tolerance band in design objectives to account for fabrication deviations caused by multilayer coating thickness variations. Compare theoretical design curve against measured transmittance with tolerance band to verify actual spectral modulation performance.
